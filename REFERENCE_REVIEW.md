# Reference and outline revision — 13 November 2026

Scope: the 18 additional entries only. Original 40 entries preserved. Every entry now has the same 11 section headings in the same order. The original heading Action Research Application is retained for consistency.

References support different types of claims: definitions, procedures, measurement, empirical outcomes, or policy implementation. Sources are cited within the content. Proposed local research questions, measures, and indicators are explicitly labeled. No direct local intervention evidence is implied.

| Entry | Sources | Evidence and limits |
|---|---:|---|
| Professional Learning Communities (PLC) | 2 | Vescio et al. (2008) reviewed 11 studies of PLCs and reported promising findings for teaching practice and student learning. They also noted that few studies went beyond self-reported benefits. This supports examining actual classroom work alongside teachers’ perceptions. |
| Five Practices of Effective School Leadership | 2 | Grissom et al. (2021) synthesized 219 studies and identified instruction-focused interaction, productive climate, collaboration and professional learning, and personnel/resource management as important practices. Their four-practice synthesis overlaps with, but is not identical to, the earlier five-practice account. |
| Model for Improvement and PDSA | 2 | Taylor et al. (2014) reviewed healthcare studies using PDSA and found inconsistent application and reporting of core methodological features. The review supports documenting predictions, small tests, repeated cycles, and data over time rather than merely naming the method. |
| ASCA National Model | 2 | Carey et al. (2012) examined rural and suburban Nebraska high schools using correlational and regression analyses. More complete implementation of ASCA-related program features was associated with improved student outcomes. The observational design does not establish causation. |
| CASEL Social and Emotional Learning Framework | 2 | Durlak et al. (2011) synthesized 213 school-based universal SEL programs and reported benefits across social-emotional and other outcomes. These findings concern implemented programs, not the CASEL diagram by itself. Program design and implementation matter. |
| Holland RIASEC Career Interest Framework | 2 | Rounds et al. (2021) bring together research on the O*NET Interest Profiler, including scale development, reliability, validity, scoring, and links to occupations. This supports its intended use in career exploration when the appropriate version and instructions are followed. |
| 5S Workplace Organization | 2 | Kanamori et al. (2016) reviewed 15 empirical healthcare studies and described reported improvements in safety, efficiency, and patient-centeredness. The heterogeneous studies provide context-specific application evidence, not a guaranteed effect size for school offices. |
| SIPOC Process Mapping | 2 | Amorim et al. (2024) applied SIPOC in a school cooperative to map administrative, financial, and pedagogical processes. The authors reported that the tool helped identify activities, inputs, outputs, suppliers, and users. This is a documented educational application. |
| Business Process Model and Notation (BPMN) | 3 | Reijers and Mendling (2011) investigated process-model comprehension using students and a replication with professional modelers. Both characteristics of readers and characteristics of models affected understanding. This supports checking comprehension rather than assuming a diagram is self-explanatory. |
| Building Blocks of Financial Capability | 3 | The CFPB’s 2016 report develops a model of financial capability from research on development and financial learning. Its 2018 measurement guide identifies age-appropriate questions and existing assessment approaches for the three building blocks. |
| OECD/EU Financial Competence Framework for Children and Youth | 2 | The OECD/EU framework specifies financial competencies; it is not itself a teaching program. OECD’s PISA 2022 financial literacy report provides assessment findings and examines associations between students’ experiences and performance. |
| CFPB Financial Well-being Framework | 3 | The CFPB’s 2015 report developed a consumer-informed definition from literature, expert input, and interviews. Its 2017 technical report documents scale development, scoring, and evidence concerning reliability and validity. |
| Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction 2015–2030 | 2 | The 2023 UN midterm review examines progress and challenges in implementing Sendai through government-led and multi-stakeholder review. It provides policy implementation findings rather than an experimental test of school interventions. |
| Comprehensive School Safety Framework 2022–2030 | 2 | GADRRRES’s 2024 policy survey documents the status and gaps of comprehensive school safety. Participation was voluntary and findings are not globally representative. The report distinguishes policy commitments from implementation. |
| Enhanced Vulnerability and Capacity Assessment (EVCA) | 2 | IFRC’s EVCA guidance sets out preparation, assessment, planning, and implementation/learning stages and identifies participatory tools. These sources document intended methodology and practical use; they do not constitute an independent effectiveness trial. |
| DPSIR Environmental Analysis Framework | 2 | Tscherning et al. (2012) analyzed 21 DPSIR studies and emphasized knowledge integration, stakeholder involvement, and presentation of alternatives for decision support. Their findings caution against treating the framework as a predetermined solution. |
| UNESCO Green School Quality Standard | 2 | Boeve-de Pauw et al. (2015) studied 2,413 learners in 51 Swedish schools and examined relationships between education for sustainable development and sustainability consciousness. This is related educational evidence, not an evaluation of UNESCO’s later 2024 standard. |
| Planetary Boundaries Framework | 2 | Richardson et al. (2023) updated the scientific assessment of nine planetary boundaries using Earth-system evidence. This supports the framework’s scientific content and discussion of interactions; it is not a study of classroom effectiveness. Its numerical status findings refer to the 2023 assessment. |

## Verification limits
Some journal sources were accessible through publisher abstracts or author/institutional records, not full text. Summaries are restricted to the accessible findings. This is a focused reference review, not a systematic review or an exhaustive bibliography. Where the cited sources do not establish school-level effectiveness, the entry says so.
