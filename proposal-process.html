Proposal Writing Process

A general sequence for planning and writing your proposal. Revisit earlier stages as evidence or feedback changes your thinking.

Ready to write? Open Proposal Builder →
1. Start with baseline evidence2. Define the problem and its relevance3. Review what is already known4. Develop the framework and choose an approach5. Write questions and match the methods6. Plan safeguards and implementation7. Assemble the proposal8. Review, revise, and prepare for submission
01

Start with baseline evidence

Gather existing assessment summaries, attendance patterns, observations, or other relevant records. Identify the group, period, measure, and comparison. Describe the actual gap without assuming its cause.

Prepare · A short baseline statement supported by aggregate or de-identified evidence.

02

Define the problem and its relevance

Narrow the problem to something you can investigate within your role, time, and setting. Explain who could benefit. Check the applicable professional standard and KRA or performance objective, then relate the inquiry to a research priority.

Prepare · A focused problem statement, scope, and explanation of alignment.

03

Review what is already known

Search credible studies and available literature. Compare their findings, methods, participants, and conditions. Ask whether the evidence supports, qualifies, or challenges your proposed response. Identify what remains uncertain in your own setting. Keep citation details for the final proposal.

Prepare · A synthesis of related findings and the gap your study will address.

04

Develop the framework and choose an approach

Explain how the key concepts relate. For action research, compare feasible responses and justify the intervention, activities, and expected outcome. For applied research without an intervention, explain the relationships or issue you will investigate and the decision the findings could inform.

Prepare · A clear framework and a reasoned choice of approach.

05

Write questions and match the methods

Write specific research questions. For each question, identify the data, participants or units, instrument, collection procedure, and analysis. Select a design that can answer the questions and describe its limits. Include a hypothesis only where it fits the design and required format.

Prepare · Research questions matched to a feasible data collection and analysis plan.

06

Plan safeguards and implementation

Describe permissions, participant information and consent or assent where applicable, confidentiality, storage, and access to data. Plan the schedule, responsibilities, costs, and resources. Explain how results could be shared and used. Consult your coordinator about required reviews before data collection.

Prepare · An ethics plan, work plan, budget if applicable, and dissemination and utilization plan.

07

Assemble the proposal

Use the required format to arrange the background, questions, framework, methods, work plan, and other sections. Add a precise working title, references, and relevant instruments or supporting documents. The Proposal Builder can organize your answers into a Word draft.

Prepare · An editable proposal with its required supporting materials.

08

Review, revise, and prepare for submission

Read across the whole proposal. Check that the baseline, problem, questions, approach, instruments, and analysis describe the same study. Verify factual claims and citations. Obtain feedback, revise the draft, and confirm the current submission instructions and attachments.

Prepare · A reviewed proposal ready for the applicable submission and evaluation process.

Use the sequence to plan your work; arrange the final document according to the template required by your school, division, or research call.
